# Kumori decisions

**2026-11-22. WebGPU rather than WebGL2.** A WebGL2 mock with rule-based drops looked good still but not under the finger. Compute shaders let the water be simulated. Pixels alone would have looked the same in either.

**2026-11-22. A particle fluid rather than a height field.** A thin-film height field is the textbook choice but is fourth order, needs tiny time steps, and smears contact lines. Double-density relaxation is cheap (well under 1 ms per step here) and gives crisp drops. The trade is that drop size is quantised by particle size.

**2026-11-22. Finer particles after the first test.** The first version had drops about 15 mm wide in real terms and tracks like paint strokes. Particle spacing went down by 0.7, and edge particles now cling to random defect sites, which is what makes a running drop shed a tail.

**2026-11-22. Focus on the glass, not the street.** The background is always out of focus, as in a photograph of a rainy window. It hides that the street is procedural, and the sharp version appears only inside drops.

**2026-11-22. Adaptive resolution lowers pixels only.** Reducing particles would visibly remove water.

**2026-11-22. Fog regrows in staggered eight-frame steps.** Tracks never fogged back over: the per-frame increment was smaller than rgba16float can represent near 0.5 and rounded to zero. rgba32float would also fix it but costs bandwidth and filtering support on some devices.

**2026-11-22. Fog uses warped gradient noise, not value noise.** Bernardo noticed regrowing fog looked squarish. Value noise sits on a screen-aligned lattice, and thresholding it (patches left in tracks) shows the squares. Fog also now grows faster beside existing fog and diffuses slightly, the glass reads the fog map with B-spline filtering, and bead sites use their whole cell. The eight-frame stagger is hashed rather than ordered for the same reason.

**2026-11-22. Lighter blurred passes; default pixel budget 3.2M, was 4.3M.** True frame rate was 22 fps at 2623x1639 with other apps holding the GPU at 82-100%. Street, lens, fog map and surface blur now run at lower resolution, which the defocus hides. Same load afterwards: 33 fps at 2263 wide, 44 fps at the 1.3M adaptive floor. Uncontended frame rate has not been measured. Adaptive resolution only steps down; stepping back up is not built.

**2026-11-22. Letters are a mask, not a fingertip.** Driving the real finger along the strokes was the obvious route, but its water would make every letter drip away before anyone touched it. The mask clears fog and beads directly and leaves about a fifth of the swept bead water as a ridge.

**2026-11-22. Melting letters add water.** Released particles are not taken from anywhere, and each letter gets a minimum (about 620 particles where strokes allow) so that sparse ones such as 辺 run rather than only bead. `ledger()` counts them under `spawned`; conservation checks should be done with no letter melting.

**2026-11-22. Tree motion is closed form.** Petals and sway are functions of age and seed in the vertex shader rather than a compute simulation. It costs no state and cannot drift. The price is that petals do not react to each other or to fingers, only to a shared wind.

**2026-11-22. The tree ignores street depth.** It is drawn over the street with no depth test against it; its canopy passes through where the facade would be. At this blur it does not read as wrong.

**2026-11-22. Petal light computed once for both faces, eight lanterns, 680-line layer.** Cut the tree's cost from about 0.92 to 0.55 of the street pass (ratio used because the GPU was busy with other work). Measured frame rates that day, same contention: 2263 wide, 35 fps without the tree and about 20 with it, before and after the trim; 584x568, 49 without and 36 with. Below 30 fps the simulation runs slower than real time, because a frame takes at most two steps.

**2026-11-22. Wind became an event, not a slider.** Bernardo saw petals drag along with the mouse: their offset was finger speed times age, so it changed the instant the finger did. This reverses part of the earlier closed-form decision: falling petals are now simulated. Checked with `kumori.petals()`: mean sideways speed unchanged at finger-up, rising to a peak about two seconds later, then falling. This was measured after the final tuning, in a small square-ish pane.

**2026-11-22. Speed pass.** Throughput at 2263 wide with the tree up and an otherwise idle GPU: 13.4 to 7.3 ms per frame (one step), 18.5 to 9.2 ms (two steps), 8.6 ms with a finger down. Largest wins: fog noise only on a texel's own turn and only where needed, bloom at quarter size, street in tiles, half-rate fog and beads when idle.

**2026-11-22. Correction to the two performance entries above.** They blame other apps for an 82-100% busy GPU. With Kumori closed the GPU read 0%. The load was most likely test frames still queued from `advance()`, so the frame rates quoted there were not under outside contention as claimed.

**2026-11-22. The pointer is a drop of water.** Bernardo could not see the mouse. A blur was the other idea, but fog is already blurred so it would vanish there. A drop bends the light, so it shows on fog and on clear glass. It is larger and rounder than the real drops and has a fixed window glint.

**2026-11-22. Animals on the CPU, surfaces on the GPU.** About 80 animals cost 0.23 ms a step in JavaScript. A compute version would have made perching, fingers and the bear much harder to write for no visible gain. Parametric surfaces instead of meshes keep the project free of assets.

**2026-11-22. The bear is a moon bear.** 熊 is the "bear" in Bernardo; tsukinowaguma live in the hills around Kyoto, and the pale crescent reads in the dark. A distance field gives a soft, furry silhouette that a lofted mesh did not promise. It is the dearest single thing to draw, so its bounds skip head and legs where they cannot matter.

**2026-11-22. Night light for the plants.** The first reeds and rapeseed were lit evenly and looked like noon. Foregrounds are now in shade, light pools, and a path is left open down the middle of the field. The shading is a heuristic, not shadowing.

**2026-11-22. Cost with everything awake.** GPU and encoding per one-step frame at 2263 wide: 6.0 ms with nothing growing, 10.5 ms with all five, 11.9 ms with the bear close. Measured live at 2560x1600 on the M2: 60 fps in all three. Fans and battery were not checked.

**2026-11-23. No cranes at the shore.** Bernardo didn't like the cranes that came with 辺, so the shore is now reeds, plumes and water only. `growth.js` no longer spawns or draws them. `cranes()` in `creatures.js` and `vsCrane`/`fsCrane` in `life.wgsl` are left in place, unused, in case they come back in another form; delete them in the final pass if not.

**2026-11-23. Koi and birds leave sooner.** Bernardo felt the fish and birds stayed too long. Their hold dropped from 60 s to 25 s (`HOLD` in `growth.js`), so each visit is about half a minute with the leaving. The reeds, rapeseed and tree still hold 60 s. The bear still comes when all five are touched within a few seconds of each other; touching them slowly, more than about 25 s from the first fish or bird, now misses it.

**2026-11-23. Lanterns and bamboo instead of koi and birds.** Bernardo wanted something more delicate than the koi and liked the scenery changes more than the animals. 流 is now a river with floating paper lanterns (toro nagashi), 瑠 a bamboo grove like Arashiyama's. The river runs down the rapeseed field's open path so the two can be awake together; the bamboo stands on both banks. Bamboo is stateless like the reeds; lanterns are simulated on the CPU because they react to fingers, wind, each other and the bear. Fireflies, wisteria and a willow were the other options offered; fireflies would fit on the same river later. Koi, bird and crane code was deleted (a copy is in the session scratchpad, not in the project). Growth-pass cost at a 925x900 pane: lanterns 0.7 ms, bamboo 0.9 ms; all five with the bear 7.0 ms; 60 fps.

**2026-11-23. Fireflies instead of lanterns; a realistic bamboo grove.** Bernardo didn't love the lanterns and asked for fireflies, and for everything shown to be "super realistic". 流 is now a narrower stream with grassy banks and Genji fireflies (yellow-green, ~2 s flashes, loosely synchronising, dark when startled). The bamboo was rebuilt after real moso groves: node scar and ridge, white bloom under nodes on young culms, olive older culms with lichen, clinging sheaths at the foot, sheathed tips while growing, branches and hanging leaf fans, a leaf-litter floor, shiba-gaki fences with rails, and uplights behind the fences (Arashiyama's lit evenings), which is what makes night bamboo read as real. Dimming the street lanterns while the fireflies are out is a stylisation, kept mild. Cost at a 925x900 pane: bamboo 2.9 ms (was 0.9), fireflies and stream 0.8 ms, all five with the bear 10.0 ms (was 7.0); 60 fps there, but not measured on a full-size screen, where the growth layer is wider.

**2026-11-23. Back to a few lanterns, and birds instead of bamboo.** Bernardo found the fireflies too many and not real when they glowed, and asked for a few realistic lanterns with a soft glow that move slowly, and for the birds back in place of the bamboo, fewer and hyper-realistic. Now: five lanterns at ~8 cm/s, with physically motivated paper light (flame distance and angle, bounce, washi fibre) and a faint halo; six life-size (16 cm) ōruri, two of them brown females, which fly perch to perch and look about rather than flock (ōruri do not flock). At life size and night light they are small on screen; that is deliberate, for realism. The street-lantern dimming used for the fireflies was removed. Fireflies and bamboo code are kept only in the session scratchpad. Growth cost with all five and the bear at a 925x900 pane: 8.6 ms.

**2026-11-23. Snow for 流; more birds.** The slow lanterns still didn't work for Bernardo; of willow, snow, maple leaves and mist he chose snow. The stream and lanterns are gone. Falling flakes are stateless sprites; settled snow is shading in the street shader driven by one number, not geometry, so it costs a few lines rather than a new pass (the street's full redraw went from about 2.7 to 4.5-6.3 ms, but only a quarter of it is redrawn each frame). Flakes are drawn as 1/90 s streaks: longer exposures read as rain. Their sideways wind push is a displacement that relaxes when the gust dies, not carried motion. Birds went from 6 to 12 at Bernardo's request. With everything awake and the bear close the growth pass measured 13.9 ms on alternate frames at a 925x900 pane; live rate stayed 60 fps there.

**2026-11-23. Settled snow starts from nothing.** The first version made the street's edges white as soon as any snow had fallen, because the edge and noise terms were added to the amount rather than setting where it appears. Now each spot has its own threshold (edges and stone tops low, joints higher, the trodden middle highest) and whitens only once the snowfall so far passes it, starting as a thin dusting. Full cover now takes about thirty seconds, the length of the fall.

**2026-11-23. Photographic pass on flowers and snow.** Bernardo asked for flowers, snow and effects to look "as if they were a picture". Looking at full-resolution crops showed the biggest loss was not the shapes but softness: the growth layer was 680 rows and laid over the glass with a deliberate blur. It is now 1000 rows and composited at mip 0 with a smaller spread. Cherry blossoms got Somei-yoshino colouring (near white, pink at the base, a heart that reddens with age), fan veins, crinkle, translucent edges and stamens (one ray-drawn quad per open flower). Rapeseed florets are now true crucifer flowers (four clawed petals, a gap between, stamens, pistil), buds are closed ovoids, stems carry seed pods, and the yellow is a clear lemon rather than ochre. Snowflakes get a photographic depth of field (focus ~3 m): near ones become soft lens discs with a faint bright rim, those near focus are small irregular clumps. Cost at a 2479x1549 canvas with everything awake and the bear close: growth pass 13.8 ms on alternate frames, live 60 fps. Not yet given the same pass: reeds and plumes, water, butterflies, birds, bear.

**2026-11-23. The bear is gone.** Bernardo didn't like it. Waking all five no longer calls anything extra; each growth simply leaves on its own clock. The uniform slots it used (world[4], bear, thin[1]) stay in the layout, zeroed, to avoid reshuffling offsets. `bear.wgsl` and the bear code are kept only in the session scratchpad.

**2026-11-23. No credit line on screen.** Bernardo hated the small "Stroke order: KanjiVG, CC BY-SA 3.0" text, so it is gone from the page. KanjiVG's licence still asks for attribution, so it now lives in the page source (a comment and a `credits` meta tag) and in `CREDITS.md`, which also notes that `assets/strokes.json` is shared under the same licence. If the piece is ever published, a hover or "about" credit would be the safer form.

**2026-11-23. Hosted free on a Cloudflare Worker.** Bernardo wants it on his phone without paying, so it follows the movie-night pattern: public GitHub repo `bsana1/kumori`, connected to Cloudflare's free tier, redeploying on every push to `main`. `kumori.pages.dev` already belonged to an unrelated manga reader, and Bernardo created a Worker (static assets) rather than a Pages project, which worked with no config file. Live at https://kumori.bernardo-sana.workers.dev. There is no build step, so the repo root is served as is; `.git` is not exposed. Phones and Safari WebGPU are still untested.

**2026-11-23. A still while it wakes.** Opening the page showed black for about 2 s on an M2, and longer on phones. Timing showed the JavaScript is done within ~0.1 s; nearly all the wait is the GPU running the 14 s head start of simulation (`?t=`), which gives the glass its rivulets. Rather than give that up, `index.html` now shows `assets/first.jpg` (the real first frame, captured with no letters, 720 px, 57 KB, preloaded) and fogs it over in CSS: a heavily blurred copy and a grey haze fade in over ~5 s. Once the first real frame has finished on the GPU (and at least 1.4 s in, so a fast load doesn't flash), `body.live` dissolves the still into the live window over 1.6 s. If WebGPU is missing, the still stays behind the message. If the scene changes a lot, the still should be recaptured. Rejected: a CSS-only drawing of the street (never quite matched), and cutting the head start (instant, but the glass starts without rivulets).

**2026-11-23. Visitor counts with Cloudflare Web Analytics.** Bernardo wants to see how many people open it. Cloudflare Web Analytics is free and uses no cookies, so no consent banner is needed; Pages has a one-click switch for it, but on a Worker the beacon script goes in `index.html` by hand (the token is public by design). The Worker's own request metrics were rejected as a visitor count because one visit fetches about 25 files. Visitors with ad blockers are not counted.

**2026-11-23. An on-screen startup report.** It didn't start on Bernardo's phone and a phone has no console to read. `index.html` now keeps a startup log from the first moment (browser, screen, WebGPU adapter and limits, each startup step, GPU errors, uncaught errors, device loss, first-second frame rate) and shows it at the bottom of the screen with `?diag`, or by itself if the window isn't alive after 20 s, so it can be screenshotted. It is plain text in the page; nothing is sent anywhere.

**2026-11-23. The head start is fed to the GPU in chunks.** On an iPhone 14 (Safari) the startup report showed `InvalidStateError: Unable to begin compute pass` in water.js. The 14 s head start was 840 separate submits queued in one go (each step needs its own uniforms, so they can't share a submit), and Safari on iOS stops being able to open command buffers at around 64 in flight. Warm-up now runs from the frame loop, 24 steps at a time, each chunk waited for with `onSubmittedWorkDone`; the loading still covers it. On the M2 it takes the same ~1.8 s as before.

**2026-11-23. Falling and running things follow the phone's tilt.** Bernardo asked for the animations to follow the phone, e.g. snow following a slight tilt. Water on the glass, snow and petals now take "down" from the orientation sensor (details in ARCHITECTURE.md, "Tilt"). Considered: turning the whole scene with the phone (physically truer, since the camera turns, but the street would tip and the letters would stay put, which reads as broken); devicemotion's accelerationIncludingGravity (signs differ between iOS and Android, so orientation angles are used instead). Snow uses an accumulated drift rather than a tilt angle in the shader, because a stateless flake placed by angle would sweep sideways across its whole fall the moment the phone tips. Lying flat, the last clear direction is kept. Desktop testing only fed fake readings; the real feel needs the phone. `?timerclock` drives frames from a timer, for testing in a hidden browser pane.

**2026-11-23. Growths stay about half as long.** Bernardo found they stayed too long. Hold (seconds from waking until the fade begins) went from 60/30/60/30/60 to 30/20/30/20/35 for 辺 reeds, 流 snow, 菜 field, 瑠 birds, 土 tree (the tree, his favourite, keeps a little more), and fades from 7-9 s to 6-8 s. Snow settles faster (0.036 → 0.05 a second) so the street still whitens in the shorter fall.

**2026-11-24. The address is kumori-bernardosana.pages.dev; the Worker is gone.** Bernardo wanted his name in the address. Pages subdomains allow only letters, digits and dashes (no `kumori.bernardosana`), `kumori.pages.dev` belongs to someone else, and a Pages project can't be renamed; Cloudflare also lets a GitHub repo feed only one Pages project. So `kumori-window` was deleted (never shared) and the repo now deploys to the Pages project `kumori-bernardosana`, https://kumori-bernardosana.pages.dev, which the preview tags and Web Analytics point at. The Worker (kumori.bernardo-sana.workers.dev) was deleted too: LinkedIn can't reach workers.dev, and nothing else needed it.

**2026-11-25. The koi are back, with the reeds.** Bernardo's son Luca loved the koi, so they return. They join 辺 (the sheet of water with reeds) rather than replacing anything: they swim through the mist above the water, as before, and gather at a finger on the glass, which suits a child. 流 stays snow. The code is the original koi (CPU movement in `creatures.js`, surface-evaluated bodies with kohaku, sanke, gold, showa and tancho markings in `life.wgsl`), restored from the session backup, minus the bear avoidance. There are 9 on a wide screen and at least 6 on a phone, and they leave with the reeds (30 s hold).

**2026-11-25. 桜梅桃李 instead of the name.** Bernardo found the phrase ōbaitōri, "each blooms in its own way": cherry, plum, peach and sumomo each flower in their own season and fashion. It fits the piece better than his name and, unlike the invented reading of 辺流菜瑠土, is a real expression. Each letter grows its own tree, and each brings one companion from a classic pairing so the snow, birds and koi stay: 梅 plum in falling snow (雪中梅), 桃 peach with the blue birds, 李 sumomo over the koi pond, 桜 the cherry as before. The rapeseed field is dropped for now. Built one tree at a time; the plum came first. Its form and flowers are a judgement from reference knowledge of ume (gnarled, zigzag, upright whips, single five-petalled flowers close to the twig, showy stamens), not from photographs. Four new KanjiVG files were downloaded with Bernardo's permission; `tools/strokes.py` lists the characters.
